One driver, a family of chips

A graphics driver is the software that lets games draw on your device's graphics chip. I build and test each of my drivers on devices I own. Players then try them on their own phones and handhelds, and some of those work too.

This page shows, for each driver, the graphics chip family it targets and every chip it has been seen running on, with a link to the proof.

At a glance

Each driver targets one family of graphics chips. The number is how many different chips in that family and beyond it have been seen running the driver so far. Tap a driver to see the chips and the proof.

What the labels mean

  • Built on

    A device I own and test every release on.

  • Works

    Passed the FauxVkTest driver check or finished its speed test, or a player reports playing games with it.

  • Partly works

    The driver loads, but some tests failed or did not finish.

  • Does not work

    Someone tried it on this device and it failed.

  • Not tested yet

    Same kind of graphics chip, so it may work. Nobody has reported back.

The drivers

Turnip is for phones and handhelds with a Qualcomm Snapdragon chip (Adreno graphics). PanVK is for chips with Arm Mali graphics, used by MediaTek, Google Tensor, Rockchip, Samsung Exynos, and Unisoc.

Since v1.0.0, PanVK is one driver with one name: the same file for every Mali device, which detects the chip it runs on and turns on only the fixes that chip needs.

How to use one

  1. Download the driver. It is one ZIP file. Do not unpack it.

  2. Open an emulator or game launcher that can load custom drivers (for example Winlator, GameNative, or Eden), find its graphics driver setting, and choose the ZIP file.

  3. Want to check it first, or add your device to this page? Run a driver check in FauxVkTest and upload the result to FauxVkDB.

These drivers come with no support. A driver can do better than your device's own driver in one game and worse in another. Your device's own driver stays installed, and every other app keeps using it.
