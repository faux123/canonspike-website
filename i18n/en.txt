One driver, <span class="grad">a family of chips</span>
A graphics driver is the software that lets games draw on your device's graphics chip. I build each of my drivers on one device I own. Players then try them on their own phones and handhelds, and some of those work too.
This page shows, for each driver, the graphics chip family it targets and every chip it has been seen running on, with a link to the proof.
At a glance
Each driver targets one family of graphics chips. The number is how many different chips in that family and beyond it have been seen running the driver so far. Tap a driver to see the chips and the proof.

The drivers
Turnip is for phones and handhelds with a Qualcomm Snapdragon chip (Adreno graphics). PanVK is for chips with Arm Mali graphics, used by MediaTek, Google Tensor, Rockchip, Samsung Exynos, and Unisoc.
Since v1.0.0, the four PanVK downloads hold the same driver: one file that detects the Mali chip it runs on and turns on only the fixes that chip needs. Each one keeps its own download, so pick the one for your chip as before. From now on, all four get every new release at the same time.

How to use one
Download the driver. It is one ZIP file. Do not unpack it.
Open an emulator or game launcher that can load custom drivers (for example Winlator, GameNative, or Eden), find its graphics driver setting, and choose the ZIP file.
Want to check it first, or add your device to this page? Run a driver check in <a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.faux.vktest" rel="noopener">FauxVkTest</a> and upload the result to <a href="https://fauxvkdb.canonspike.com/">FauxVkDB</a>.
These drivers come with no support. A driver can do better than your device's own driver in one game and worse in another. Your device's own driver stays installed, and every other app keeps using it.
